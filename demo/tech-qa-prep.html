The questions you will actually get

Ordered by how likely they are. The pill under each answer names the file to open if a judge wants to see it.

01 How do you stop it hallucinating a legal position?

Say: “Three separate constraints, and they’re independent. First, the bill summaries come only from the Explanatory Statement — we don’t ask the model what a bill means, we extract the section Parliament already wrote. Second, every flag must carry a verbatim quote from the client document, and it’s marked found or inferred so the lawyer sees which claims rest on the text and which on reasoning. Third, the system prompt tells it that topical overlap is never impact, and we test that with deliberate traps.”

Then show it: on the sweep slide, “20 documents scanned and left unflagged.” That is the constraint working, not marketing.

If pushed further: the model can still mis-summarise why a clause is exposed. What it cannot do is invent a clause — the quote is checked against the document text, and a lawyer reading the quote catches a bad rationale in seconds. That is the whole reason the quote is mandatory rather than optional.

02 What is actually scraped live, and what is faked?

Say: “Parliament’s Bills Introduced page is server-rendered, so we scrape it live and parse each bill’s PDF for its commencement clause. PAIR Search, eGazette and SSO are JavaScript applications or block non-browser clients — we verified that on 5 September. For those we serve a cached snapshot through the same parser interface, so wiring a headless browser is a swap of the fetch function, not a rewrite.”

This is a strength, say it confidently. The docstring at the top of parliament.py records exactly this, with the verification date. Offer to open it. Teams that pretend everything is live get caught; you documented the boundary before anyone asked.
scrapers/parliament.py:1–8Scraper dev

03 What happens when the parliament site changes its HTML?

Say: “The scrape returns an empty list rather than throwing, and we fall back to the cached snapshot automatically — the UI drops the ‘live’ tag to ‘cached snapshot’, so the user is told. Parsing is one regex over flattened text in one function, so a DOM change is a contained fix.”

Don’t claim a second AI auto-repairs the scraper. That’s in an early script draft but it isn’t built, and “we’d monitor and fix it” is a perfectly good answer. Say it as roadmap if you want: “the obvious next step is a canary that alerts when the parse returns zero bills.”
scrapers/parliament.py:22–37Scraper dev

04 How do you know it’s accurate? Have you measured it?

Say: “Yes — eval.py runs every amendment across every client and scores against hand-labelled ground truth. It reports recall, precision, exact category match for high vs moderate, and a separate count of false-positive traps passed. The traps are documents that are topically adjacent but genuinely unaffected — a PDPA consent form against a PDPA breach-window change.”

Before you present, actually run it and know the current numbers: python backend/eval.py. It sweeps every amendment × every client, so it costs roughly $0.60 and takes a couple of minutes. Do not quote a number you have not seen today — the corpus changed when PivotStack was added.
backend/eval.pyBackend dev

05 Why one LLM call per client? Isn’t that expensive?

Say: “It’s about twelve-tenths of a cent per client per amendment — around eight cents to sweep our whole demo firm. For a 200-client firm it’s roughly four dollars per amendment, and amendments arrive a few times a month. Against a partner’s hourly rate that’s not the constraint.”

“We isolate per client deliberately: one client’s documents never enter another client’s prompt. That’s a confidentiality property, and it also means one bad response can’t poison the whole sweep — failures are caught per pair and reported, the sweep continues.”

The confidentiality framing is the strong half of this answer. Lead with it if the questioner sounds like a lawyer rather than an engineer.
analysis/impact.py:164–195Backend dev

06 Where does client data go? This is privileged material.

Say: “In the demo, documents sit in local JSON and the analysis call goes to a hosted model. For deployment the model is the thing that moves, not the data — a firm runs this against their own endpoint, whether that’s an enterprise agreement with a provider or a self-hosted model, inside their own environment. Nothing about the engine assumes a particular vendor; it’s one HTTP client behind one interface.”

Do not say “no client data leaves the firm” as if it were already true. Today the prompt goes to OpenRouter. The honest version — “this is the deployment model, and here’s the single file that changes” — is more convincing and cannot be caught out.

07 Why should a lawyer trust high vs moderate?

Say: “Two grounds, both in the prompt and both visible in the output. Ground one is timing: a change already in force or gazetted with a fixed date can be high; a bill still awaiting commencement caps at moderate, because the firm cannot yet be wrong about a rule that isn’t law. Ground two is clause hierarchy: the operative clause that states the superseded position is high, a clause exposed only by cross-reference is moderate.”

Then point at the sweep slide — the Why line literally names “Ground 1” and “Ground 2”. The reasoning is auditable, not a vibe.

analysis/impact.py — SYSTEMPresenter

08 Can I type my own question into it?

Say: “Yes — free text is routed to a grounded Q&A over the same corpus, so it can’t invent a client or a provision.”

Know the limit before they find it. The sweep/question router is keyword-based (qa.py:77). “Run a sweep” and “which clients are affected” route to a sweep; “Does the WFA affect any of our clients?” does not — it falls through to Q&A. It still answers from the same grounded corpus, so it degrades gracefully rather than breaking. If it happens live: “That routed to Q&A rather than a full sweep — the router is keyword-based today, an intent classifier is the fix. Let me select the amendment and run the sweep properly.” Then do exactly that.
analysis/qa.py:77–86Backend dev

09 Is your client data real?

Say: “No, and deliberately — the client corpus is entirely synthetic. The regulatory sources are real: real Bills, real provisions, scraped from Parliament. We built the corpus with known-correct answers so we could measure accuracy, which you can’t do on real files you don’t have labels for.”

Turn it into the eval answer. Synthetic data is why ground_truth.json exists and why precision and recall are measurable at all. That’s a methodology strength, not an apology.
Presenter

10 Can it handle a real 40-page contract, not a paragraph?

Say: “Uploads go through the same path — .docx, .pdf or .txt, text extracted on upload, including clauses inside tables, which is where they usually sit in an employment contract. An uploaded document is swept identically to a seeded one; there’s no special-casing in the engine.”

Honest limit: the whole document text goes into the prompt, so a very long contract will eventually hit the context window. We haven’t implemented chunking or retrieval. If asked: “Not yet — clause-level chunking is the next step, and it’s also what would let us cite clause numbers instead of quoting text.”
backend/ingest.pyFrontend dev

11 What if the model returns something malformed?

Say: “The JSON parser tolerates markdown fencing and leading prose. If a pair still fails, it’s caught, recorded in an errors list, and the sweep continues — one bad response degrades the result, it doesn’t kill it. There’s no automatic retry today; that’s a deliberate simplification, and it’s a few lines to add.”

llm.py:48–57 · impact.py:191Backend dev

12 What stops a lawyer approving everything unread?

Say: “Nothing stops a careless lawyer, and that’s true of every precedent template a firm already uses. What we change is the cost of checking: the suggestion arrives with the verbatim clause, the provision that compels it, and what happens if it’s left. Checking takes seconds. And every decision is attributed in the audit trail — that log is what a firm shows its insurer.”

Amenda sits where a junior associate sits. The approving solicitor keeps full professional responsibility.

Rehearse these three, out loud

Reading answers is not the same as saying them under pressure. Twenty minutes on these will do more than another hour of polish.

Drill 1 — the handoff

  1. Someone asks a hard engine question mid-demo. Presenter says one sentence, then hands over by name.
  2. The dev answers in under thirty seconds and hands back.
  3. Run it three times. The goal is that the handoff sounds rehearsed, because it is.

Drill 2 — “show me the code”

  1. Have the repo open in a second window before you start, not minimised behind the demo.
  2. Practise reaching impact.py’s system prompt and parliament.py’s docstring in under ten seconds each.
  3. Those two files answer half the questions on this page.

Drill 3 — the answer you don’t have

  1. Agree the exact wording now: “We haven’t built that. What we did build is X, and here’s why we prioritised it.”
  2. Then stop. Do not invent a roadmap on the spot, and do not let two people improvise different answers.
  3. The gaps most likely to come up: chunking for long documents, retry on malformed JSON, an intent classifier for the router, and automated monitoring of scraper health.

Last checks, the morning of

Run python backend/eval.py and write the numbers on your hand. The corpus changed when PivotStack was added; yesterday’s figures are stale.

Reset the demo: git checkout -- backend/data/proposals.json — otherwise the redline opens already approved and you lose the Approve moment.

Confirm .env exists on the demo machine. It’s gitignored; a fresh clone will not have it and the sweep will fail.

Check the live tag appears next to Amendments. If the venue blocks the scrape, the cached fallback is automatic — but know which one you’re showing before a judge asks.
